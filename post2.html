Burnham's Big Budget - Beleaguered

Published: November 2, 2026

I, like many other political students and commentators, was eagerly anticipating the mystical first budget announcement from Andy Burnham. I, like many others, was left disappointed by its eventual arrival. After many weeks of lead up promising a focus on NEETs and the current state of the job market, I was intrigued at how the autumn budget would approach these issues. A dramatic reconstruction of the benefit system? A radical roadmap of employability support? In the end not a word was said about the issues being faced, and the result was a largely underwhelming budget. While the Youth Employment and Skills Package was officially confirmed, it did not come with much explanation of where the money will come from. Having spoken with many Labour-sceptical voters, suspicion of the current benefit system is a large part of their aversion to Burnham. This budget was the big opportunity to show the country that he was taking a big step into his role, and committing to big changes. A move like that would have pricked many ears up. Instead, we got a largely energy focused budget that has gone down as well as energy prices. A token direct payment that will stretch as far as next week. The path forward for Burnham isn't as bright as he may have thought when he was having his summer of fun - chipping away at parking fine and public transport annoyances. His chance to make his big stand has been missed. Now all that is left is to wait to see Burnham's response to both the Timms and Milburn reviews next year and see if Burnham's Britain will start to look profitable...
