Skydance's Motives Leave DC in Disarray

Published: November 24, 2026

During David Ellison’s acquisition of WarnerBros. Discovery, promises were made to fans that James Gunn and Peter Safran’s oversight of the newly established DCU (DC cinematic universe) would persevere. Within weeks they have been fired. Is this a routine management change, or does it suggest some ulterior motives within Hollywood?

Gunn has always been divisive, from writing a divisive script for 2002’s Scooby-Doo that had to be toned down, to getting fired and rehired by Marvel for old tweets. But in recent years, his work has been criticised for being too political.

2025’s “Superman” movie sees the titular character ponder the ethics of a naturalised American citizen interfering in international conflicts without facing consequence. This is a fairly common trope, seen in 2016’s “Captain America: Civil War” too. The difference in “Superman’s” case is timing.

“Superman” released two years after the October 7th invasion, the beginning of the Gaza war, and subsequent genocide. The conflict in the movie sees an American billionaire (Lex Luthor) backing a powerful nation in its conquest of its neighbour, in order to profit off the invasion and gain territory. James Gunn has denied any intentional parallels to real world conflicts, but the similarities are present, whether on purpose or not.

The heroes of course are triumphant, the oppressing nation’s president is explicitly killed by Hawkgirl, and the local village defending their nation’s border are freed from the faceless military opposing them. But for some, this ending might cause some upset.

Billionaires empathising with Lex Luthor’s backing of the conflict might feel downtrodden that this investment hasn’t come up trumps. Specifically, the new owners of DC, the Ellison family.

On October 6th Paramount Skydance (now just ‘Skydance’) acquired WarnerBros. Discovery for around $81billion. This was funded largely through Skydance owner David Ellision and his father, Oracle owner Larry Ellision, as well as large contributions from Saudi Arabia, the UAE, and Qatar, who now own around 11% of the company respectively. A few weeks after the merger saw on-off trillionaire Elon Musk invest $1billion into the company for a 3% share. This deal included the film rights for DC, which had been left in the hands of Gunn and Safran before the merger.

It can’t be said that James Gunn’s DC cinematic universe was breaking records, with its second entry “Supergirl” losing $100million. Critical success has been found on the small screen with “Peacemaker” and “Lanterns”, but with a merger costing $81billion, streaming entries aren’t big money earners. Budget horror film “Clayface” has turned a profit but it’s a slow and steady start for the DCU – is this a reason to abandon the direction that Gunn and Safran have been building?
