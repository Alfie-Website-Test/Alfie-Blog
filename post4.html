Hiking in Gran Canaria - An Immigration Battleground

Published: November 23, 2026

I spent this past week hiking 75km across Gran Canaria, an island often called a “miniature continent” thanks to its diverse biomes. Every leg of our journey felt like a whole different nation, but there was a unifying feature that was ever-present no matter where we found ourselves: the political banners and placards ready for the upcoming election. Walking through the island seeing “Defiende Canarias” slogans and love heart logos was a constant reminder of the political reality that surrounds us.

This was especially pertinent as while we were walking, El Hierro (another island making up the Canary Islands), was facing an influx of immigrant boat arrivals larger than they had capacity for. This led to Spain declaring a state of emergency on the archipelago. This is not a new phenomenon, with the Canary Islands being a very common destination for West African migrants, but it is one of the deadliest routes. This often leads to ebbs and flows of arrivals and rescues, depending on sea and weather conditions. Saturday and Sunday (November 14th and 15th) saw the largest single-day figure of Migrant rescues across the Canary Islands in 30 years at over 2500.

Paired with the illegal entry of over 10,000 people to Spain’s North African territory of Cueta in July, it is clear these events will influence the outcome of Spain’s general election on the 29th of November.

As news from back home continues to focus on the housing if immigrants in hotels, it was clear that Spain will have to find a solution too. With the far-right Vox party leader speaking in Tenerife just after the immigration news broke, he made bold claims - “Whoever enters Spain illegally will leave Spain and will never be made legal”. The Canaries in total hold 15 seats in the Spanish congress, so it is a crucial battle ground, whether Sanchez’ Social Democratic PSOE have enough to convince the Spanish voters to stick with him will be eagerly anticipated.
